# Model answers

1. Symmetric (zero point 0): simpler, cheaper integer arithmetic, wastes range on one-sided distributions (post-ReLU activations), where asymmetric with a zero point uses all 256 levels. Per-tensor: one scale, simplest kernels, ruined by outlier channels. Per-channel: one scale per output channel for weights, near-free in compute and much lower error; standard for weights. Activations are usually per-tensor (per-token for transformers when outliers bite).
2. Run a few hundred real inputs through the model, record each activation's distribution, choose a range that minimises error: a high percentile (99.9–99.99%), MSE-optimal clipping, or entropy (KL) calibration. Min-max lets one rare outlier stretch the range, so most values fall into a few levels.
3. PTQ is enough when the int8 version's action error and closed-loop success are within your tolerance: usual for CNNs and MLP heads with per-channel weights. QAT when PTQ degrades too much (small models, low bit widths, sensitive heads): it fine-tunes with fake quantisation so weights adapt to the grid; costs a training run, data and care with batch norm and learning rates.
4. Rounding is piecewise constant, so its true gradient is zero or undefined and gives no signal. The STE treats it as the identity within the clamp range: the gradient of the loss with respect to the quantised value is used as the gradient for the unquantised value. Biased, but the quantisation error is small and bounded (half a step), so the direction is nearly right and training converges onto weights that work after rounding.
5. The first layers that see raw inputs (patch embedding), normalisation layers, attention softmax and its scores, the final action projection and small heads (gripper, flow time embedding), and anything whose outputs are compared to thresholds. They are cheap and errors there propagate or directly change actions. The big matmuls of the backbone are where the savings are.
6. Decoding a VLA on an edge GPU is usually memory-bandwidth bound (weights read for every forward pass at small batch): weight-only int4 cuts the bytes moved by 4× vs fp16 and helps latency most, with small accuracy loss using activation-aware methods. int8 weights + activations helps compute-bound parts (prefill of image tokens, large batches) when TensorRT has int8 kernels for them, but activation outliers in transformers make it harder. Measure both on the device.
