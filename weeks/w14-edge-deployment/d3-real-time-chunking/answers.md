# Model answers

1. Staleness: the new chunk is computed from an observation d steps old, so its first d actions refer to the past while the robot was executing the old chunk; using them as-is replays or contradicts what just happened. Inconsistency: the new chunk is an independent sample; a multimodal policy can pick another mode, and even within a mode its start does not match where the old chunk left the arm, so the arm jumps at the seam.
2. It generates the new chunk conditioned on the actions that will certainly be executed (the old chunk shifted by the executed steps): inpainting with guidance during flow sampling, so the new chunk continues the current motion and stays in the same mode. A soft mask gives full weight to the frozen prefix and decaying weight to the rest of the overlap, so the new chunk can deviate smoothly where the new observation says so, instead of being either fully constrained or not at all.
3. An iterative generative sampler whose intermediate denoised estimate can be guided (flow matching or diffusion), and a chunk representation aligned in time with the previous one. Deterministic regression heads (ACT's L1 head) cannot be guided this way; they use temporal ensembling or blending instead. Discrete token heads need a different (constrained decoding) approach.
4. Same checkpoint, same starts, blocked schedule, three execution modes; measure success, task time, seam discontinuities (joint velocity/acceleration spikes at switches from the logs), and with injected extra latency (50, 150, 300 ms) to see degradation curves. Pre-register the primary metric.
5. Naive async: more stale actions skipped or replayed and bigger seam jumps; eventually starvation. Temporal ensembling: averaging over more stale predictions, sluggish and can average modes into nonsense. RTC: degrades gracefully as long as the chunk is long enough to cover the latency plus the execution horizon (W12 D4), because the frozen prefix absorbs the delay.
6. Train the policy with the prefix as an explicit input (conditioning on previous actions, "training-time RTC"): no guidance at inference (cheaper, no extra hyperparameters) but requires retraining and a fixed range of delays seen in training; or simulate delays during training. Inference-time inpainting works on any pretrained flow policy without retraining.
