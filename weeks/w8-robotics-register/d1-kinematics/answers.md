# Model answers

1. One matrix product composes rotations and translations along a chain, transforms points with one multiplication, and inverts cleanly; no bookkeeping of "rotate then translate" order errors.
2. Configurations where the Jacobian loses rank: some tip directions cannot be produced by any joint velocity (fully stretched arm, wrist aligned with the shoulder axis, the tip on the pan axis). Plain pseudo-inverse IK asks for huge joint velocities there; damping trades a small tracking error for bounded steps.
3. Joint space: matches teleop data (leader arm = joint positions), no IK needed at runtime, no singularity issues, embodiment-specific. EE space: transfers across arms and camera setups, easier to reason about in task space and for language (left/up), needs IK and handling of redundancy/limits.
4. A transposed rotation is a rotation by −θ: still orthonormal with det 1; for small angles or symmetric poses it looks similar. 10-second test: apply it to a basis vector at 90° and check the right-hand rule.
5. Lever arm ~0.35 m: 2° error ≈ 0.035 rad × 0.35 m ≈ 12 mm at the tip per joint (errors can add). Enough to miss a bowl rim: why calibration matters and why policies trained on one arm's data degrade on another.
6. Constraints: joint limits, self-collision and environment collision checks, preference among redundant solutions (elbow up/down), continuity with the current configuration (no flips), velocity limits. A motion planner (or at least limit-aware, collision-checked IK with a null-space objective).
7. DLS is a local method: it follows the gradient of the position error from the start configuration and can settle in a configuration where J e ≈ 0 (the error is orthogonal to every joint motion: a local minimum or a singular, folded pose), especially near the singular stretched arm. Fixes: several restarts from different initial q, a null-space term toward a preferred posture, or solve the reachable projection analytically (point the arm at the target, stretch) as the initial guess.
