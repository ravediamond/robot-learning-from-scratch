# Model answers

1. Start from the decision: the target task matters most, so its data must not be drowned. Temperature sampling interpolates between proportional (T = 1: maximum diversity of samples, the large source dominates) and uniform over sources (T → ∞: each source equally, small ones repeated). Pick a few settings (proportional, T = 3, a fixed share like 50% ours), train each with 2–3 seeds, and choose on closed-loop success on the target task, plus a check of the other tasks you care about.
2. It is repeated many epochs while the others are not: the model memorises it (training loss on that source drops to near zero, held-out loss on that source rises), and its quirks (one operator's style, one lighting) dominate behaviour. Detect with per-source training and held-out losses and with epochs-per-source computed up front; fix with a lower weight, augmentation, or more data of that kind.
3. Train on nested subsets (25%, 50%, 100% of the task's data, same seeds and mixture otherwise), evaluate closed-loop on fixed starts, fit a curve of error vs hours, and read the slope at the current size: the marginal gain per hour. Compare with the slope of other levers (other tasks' data, a better model) to allocate the collection budget.
4. The seed-to-seed spread and the paired interval (same seeds with and without), the number of rollouts per evaluation and its CI, whether the eval protocol was blocked and identical, and whether the effect holds on more than one task or split. A −4 with a paired CI of [−9, +1] is "not resolved".
5. When the skills are shared at the level the model can transfer (visual understanding, object-centric behaviour, language grounding) and actions are expressed in a comparable space (end-effector deltas, normalised per embodiment, with an embodiment token). Test: train with and without, same total steps, measure on your task, especially in low-data regimes (10–50 demos) where transfer shows; check that it does not hurt at high data.
6. Visual and semantic variety (domain randomisation of textures, lighting, distractors), task structure, rare situations and failures that are expensive to collect, large numbers of trajectories for pretraining. Risks: unrealistic contact dynamics and timing, perfect states. Mitigate: keep the real data as the fine-tuning or high-weight source, randomise physics, tag sim samples (a domain token), use sim mostly for representation/pretraining, and check on real rollouts that adding sim does not change contact-rich behaviour for the worse.
