# Model answers

1. Raw: 640×480×3 × 30 fps ≈ 27.6 MB/s ≈ 100 GB per camera-hour, 300 GB per hour for three cameras, 3 PB for 10,000 hours: not stored raw. Compressed at ~1 Mbit/s per camera: 0.45 GB per camera-hour, ~13.5 TB in total (more at higher quality or resolution). Video dominates; state and action tables are megabytes. Cost drivers: replicas, hot vs cold tiers, egress to training clusters, and re-encodes kept for different training settings.
2. Training samples random frames across thousands of files; each costs the decode from the previous keyframe plus file opening and seeking, on the CPU, while GPUs wait. Fixes: sample contiguous windows (one decode serves many frames), re-encode training copies with short GOPs or all-intra at lower resolution, decode on GPU (NVDEC), pre-extract frames at training resolution for hot datasets, cache decoded clips, and give the loader enough CPU cores (measure frames/s per core).
3. Map-style: random access by index, true global shuffle, defined epochs: fine when data fits a filesystem with fast random access. Iterable/streaming: data read sequentially from shards (object storage, too large for random access): shuffling is local (shard order shuffled per epoch + shuffle buffer), "epoch" is approximate, and each (rank, worker) needs its own shard subset.
4. Seed everything that orders data (shard permutation per epoch, buffer RNG), derive them from (seed, epoch, rank, worker), and checkpoint the loader state: shards done, position in the current shard, buffer contents or at least its RNG state and the items consumed. Simplest robust approach: resume at shard boundaries, skipping a known number of samples, and accept a slightly different buffer order.
5. A parquet table of frames (timestamps, episode and frame indices, state, action, task index) grouped in chunked files, metadata files (info, episodes with their offsets into files, tasks, normalisation stats), and per-camera MP4 videos (AV1 by default) with many episodes per file in v3, addressed by timestamps. Video keeps storage two orders of magnitude smaller than images; parquet makes the low-dimensional data fast to scan and filter.
6. Consecutive frames of the same episode: highly correlated batches, low gradient diversity, effective batch size much smaller than nominal, and the model sees one task or one operator at a time (it can drift between them). Fix: shuffle at the shard and episode level first (interleave many episodes), make the buffer much larger than an episode, or sample windows from many episodes in parallel.
