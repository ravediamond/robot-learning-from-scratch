# Model answers

1. Deploy (policy runs, everything logged with manifests) → detect (success labels, failure-detector scores, interventions) → triage (review queue, failure taxonomy) → collect (targeted demonstrations and corrections where it fails) → curate (QA, ledger, version) → train (mixture with the new data, seeds) → evaluate (blocked, paired, against the current policy) → promote and repeat. Human cost: operators supervising and intervening, reviewers labelling (minutes per episode), demonstrators collecting, and the evaluation sessions themselves; measure each in hours per iteration.
2. They are on-policy: they cover the states the policy actually reaches and fails in, which demonstrations from the expert's own distribution never visit (the compounding-error problem). Each one shows a recovery. Biases: humans intervene late and only on visible failures, their recoveries are from awkward states with jerky corrections, interventions depend on the operator's threshold, and the pre-intervention segment is the policy's behaviour, not a demonstration (do not label it as one).
3. By expected value of the label: high failure-detector score (likely failures to tag and learn from), high uncertainty (where the detector or the success label is unsure, which also improves the detector), plus stratified random samples so that metrics stay unbiased and new failure types are found. Cap per-condition so one recurring failure does not fill the queue.
4. From the latest blocked evaluation: per start condition, the failure rate and its uncertainty; allocate in proportion to failures plus uncertainty (largest-remainder), keep a share (e.g. 20%) spread uniformly for coverage and to avoid over-fitting the evaluation's starts, and prefer corrections from interventions where the failure is a recovery problem. Pre-register the expected effect on the targeted conditions.
5. Measure each iteration with the same protocol: success on targeted and untargeted conditions, interventions per hour in deployment, with paired comparisons against the previous policy. Keep an A/A check. Compare the gain per labelling hour with the alternative (random new demos of the same volume) at least once, so the targeting itself is shown to matter.
6. Pretrain/imitate on the 10,000 hours (curated: QA, near-duplicates down-weighted, held-out sessions). Label the 100 hours with success and progress (classifier + human review), train a value function on all of it, and use the rollouts three ways: corrections from interventions (DAgger-style), advantage-conditioned training (RECAP-style: positive vs negative actions inside the rollouts), and failure detectors for deployment monitoring. Keep the 100 hours from being drowned (explicit mixture weights, epochs per source), evaluate with paired blocked real-robot comparisons, and report what the rollouts bought per hour of human time.
