# Model answers

1. Typical: MSE collision ~0.45–0.6 (CI about ±0.07), flow ~0.05–0.08. The Wilson intervals do not overlap and the gap is ~40 points with n = 200: significant. Better still: run both on the same 200 start states (same seed) and compare paired outcomes.
2. Small asymmetries (start y-noise, network imprecision) break the tie: once the robot is slightly above the axis, the learned mean action points "up" (demos there go up), and it commits. MSE successes come from starts/trajectories that drifted off the axis early; failures from those that stayed on it.
3. 1 step: the sample collapses to the conditional mean (Thursday's result) → MSE-like behavior, many collisions. Replanning every step: each new chunk is an independent sample; near the axis the policy may switch modes from one step to the next (up, down, up) and dither into the obstacle; committing to 4 steps of one chunk keeps the decision. Chunking is part of how flow policies handle multimodality.
4. Which bowl to grasp first, grasping the rim at different points, approach from left vs right of the other bowl, pause or not. Flow helps where the same observation has several valid actions; in a highly regular bowls task with consistent teleop, MSE-like heads (ACT) can be competitive: consistent with your SmolVLA ≈ VLA-JEPA result.
5. Normalization lives in several places (dataset stats, model buffers, config, the runtime), and a mismatch gives plausible-looking but wrong commands with no error. Others from this course: channels-last vs channels-first images (W2 D3), checkpoint not loaded / partial load (W3 D5), stats from the wrong split (W1 D5), degrees vs radians, action = delta vs absolute.
6. Val MSE measures distance to the demo actions; the MSE head is optimal for that metric by construction (it predicts the mean). The flow head's samples are individually valid but each far from the "average demo", so its MSE is higher while its closed-loop behavior is much better. The metric that matters is closed-loop success; val MSE ranks them wrongly here (week 6 shows this systematically).
