# Model answers

1. `mj_step` integrates the dynamics by one timestep (it computes forces, then advances qpos, qvel and time). `mj_forward` computes everything that depends on the current state (body and site positions, Jacobians, bias forces, contacts) without advancing time. Needed after writing qpos by hand (IK, resets, replaying a pose) before reading positions or Jacobians.
2. Contacts and stiff servos need small timesteps (1–2 ms) to be stable and accurate; a policy at 30–50 Hz is what real cameras and inference allow. Off by one: the simulated time per policy step is wrong by 10% (at 10 substeps), so velocities, delays and anything tuned in sim are scaled; a replay drifts from the log.
3. At rest q̇ = 0 and τ = kp(q_des − q) must equal the gravity torque g(q), so e = g(q)/kp. Remove it with gravity compensation (feed-forward g(q): needs a correct mass model, wrong with unknown payloads) or an integral term (no model needed; adds lag, wind-up and overshoot). Higher kp only shrinks it and costs stability under latency.
4. It regularises the inverse: dq = Jᵀ(JJᵀ + λ²I)⁻¹e bounds the step when JJᵀ is nearly singular, trading exactness for stability. At λ = 0 near full extension the smallest singular value goes to zero and the step in that direction explodes: huge joint velocities for a tiny Cartesian motion.
5. Use `<position>` actuators (or a motor with your own PD) with kp and kv matching the servo's internal loop, plus armature (reflected rotor inertia), damping, frictionloss, backlash if modelled, and `forcerange` for the torque limit. Unknown: the servo's internal PID gains and their units, its control period and delay, the torque-speed curve, deadband, backlash, and voltage dependence. That is what system identification (tomorrow) is for.
6. (a) Plain MuJoCo on CPU: accurate, fast for one model, easy to script thousands of short rollouts for CEM, no GPU needed. (b) A GPU-parallel simulator: Isaac Lab (PhysX, mature RL tooling, rendering) or MuJoCo MJX / MuJoCo Warp (MuJoCo's model on GPU); the choice is mostly ecosystem and contact fidelity. Genesis is fast but younger: check contact quality for your task before trusting it. Whatever you train in, validate in a second simulator before the real robot (sim-to-sim).
