# Model answers

1. Average pooling keeps "what features are present" and throws away where. Spatial softmax keeps the (x, y) location of each learned feature: object and gripper positions. For grasping, location is the whole problem, so spatial softmax (or keep the spatial feature map and attend to it).
2. At inference the batch is 1 frame and BN switches to running statistics estimated on training batches of correlated frames; any shift (lighting, a new episode's appearance) gives activations the network never saw. GroupNorm normalizes per sample, so train and test behave identically.
3. Resize keeps the whole field of view but loses fine detail (small objects, gripper fingers). Crop keeps resolution but can cut out the object or the target. Common: resize to ~224 or 96 for the top camera; for the wrist camera, center crop or resize. Check by looking at the images the model gets.
4. Each "channel" is a mix of interleaved R, G, B values from different pixels, and rows are wrapped. Still a deterministic function of the image, so a CNN can learn some correlations (brightness, coarse layout), but spatial structure and colors are scrambled: worse features, worse policy, no error.
5. Small camera-pose and object-position jitter in the training data → the policy cannot memorize exact pixel positions and becomes robust. Hurts when absolute position in the image matters and the camera is fixed and calibrated (precise placement from a top camera with a known pixel-to-world map), or for a wrist camera where a shift means a different gripper-object relation. Use small pads (4 px on 84–96 px images).
6. With 50 demos: a pretrained ResNet-18 (with GroupNorm swap, fine-tuned at low lr, or frozen with spatial softmax head) usually generalizes better to lighting and distractors; from-scratch small CNN overfits less if the scene is very constant and is faster. Decide by a quick ablation: same demos, both encoders, success on 20 held-out layouts. ACT and Diffusion Policy both default to pretrained ResNet-18.
