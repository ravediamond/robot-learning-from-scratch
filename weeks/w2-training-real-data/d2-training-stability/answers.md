# Model answers

1. ln 100 = 4.6, so 9.2 is bad: confident wrong logits at init (logit scale too large, inputs unnormalized, or label/output mismatch). 2.1 is below chance at step 0: suspicious too: label leakage into inputs, or the loss is computed on a subset/averaged wrongly, or the model is not fresh.
2. Adam divides by √v; at step 1, v is one squared gradient (bias-corrected), a very noisy estimate; small v in some coordinates → huge effective steps. Early updates also move from random init where gradients are large and inconsistent. Warmup keeps steps small until m and v are averaged over many batches. SGD has no such normalization, its step is proportional to the gradient.
3. Every step is clipped: you are effectively doing normalized-gradient descent with step lr × 1.0, and the reported lr is not your real lr. Not necessarily bad, but lr and clip are now coupled; raise the clip or lower the lr so clipping only catches spikes (clip rarely active, e.g. <5% of steps).
4. After zero_grad() grads are None (default set_to_none=True), so `clip_grad_norm_` finds nothing to clip and returns 0. No error because clipping an empty set is legal. backward() then fills full-size grads; step() applies the spike.
