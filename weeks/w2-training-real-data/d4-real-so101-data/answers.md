# Model answers

1. (a) Hold-position (action = current state) is wrong by exactly the lag: its error is the motion over k frames, so it is a weaker baseline than it looks on fast segments. (b) The policy must predict at least past the lag (commands take effect k frames later): chunks ≥ several times k. (c) From the current state alone, the policy cannot see the commands already "in flight"; giving it the last action(s) or a short history helps; or it learns a systematic lead.
2. Drop: simplest, slight data loss, safe if few. Keep: windows across the gap pair an obs with actions that are 10 frames later than they claim → wrong targets around the gap. Interpolate: fine for state/action if the motion is smooth, but camera frames are missing and cannot be invented. For evaluation episodes, never interpolate: drop or flag. Usually: drop or split the episode at the gap.
3. Many frames with "state = start pose, action = start pose" teach: at the start pose, do nothing. At test time the robot starts at that pose and never leaves it (a fixed point). Trimming removes that, but also removes the information "wait until the scene is ready"; and trimming thresholds can cut the first slow motion. Better: trim with a threshold and check a few episodes by eye.
4. File/row order depends on how files were listed and concatenated (filesystem order, parallel reads, a different glob). Never rely on implicit row order: sort by an explicit key (frame_index, timestamp) right where order matters, and assert monotonicity.
5. Raw: 61,462 frames × 2 cams × 480×640×3 B ≈ 113 GB. MP4 is typically 100–300× smaller (hundreds of MB). So you cannot keep raw frames in RAM; decoding cost dominates the loader → decode once to small resized frames (e.g. 96×96 = 27 KB/frame → ~3.4 GB for both cams) and cache.
6. (1) Episode length distribution and outliers. (2) Timestamp gaps / dropped frames per episode. (3) Idle frames at start/end. (4) Action-state lag and tracking error (follower not keeping up = overloaded servo). (5) Joint ranges and limits hit; gripper open/close events per episode (should be ≥1 for a grasp task). Plus: watch 5 random episodes as video, check camera sync (frame timestamps vs state timestamps), and check per-operator/session statistics.
