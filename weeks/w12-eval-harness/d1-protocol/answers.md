# Model answers

1. Battery voltage and motor temperature (servo torque and tracking change): interleave policies in blocks. Lighting through the day (perception): blocks again, plus fixed artificial light or curtains. Object and table wear (friction, a crushed cube): interleaving spreads it, and replace objects on a fixed schedule recorded in the log. Operator learning and fatigue (faster, cleaner resets; more lenient calls later): interleaving, blinding, written success criteria and a reset checklist.
2. They make the comparison paired: each start is a hard or easy case for every policy alike, so the variance from "which start did you get" cancels (McNemar, paired bootstrap). Enough to cover the task's variation (positions, orientations, distractors); 10–20 is common, each repeated. Too few: the result describes those starts, not the task, and the policy that happens to like them wins.
3. The labeller's expectations: borderline successes, "a glitch, let's retry", how carefully the scene is reset, when to intervene, how long to wait before calling a timeout. Blind with aliases on the run sheet, a policy-agnostic launcher, written criteria; when behaviour gives the policy away, have a second person label from video without the alias, and report agreement.
4. A broken record at analysis time is a rollout you cannot redo: the session is over, the scene has changed, nobody remembers. Validation at write time asks the operator to fix it while the evidence is in front of them, and it makes missing data visible instead of silently dropped.
5. Roughly 30–40 s per rollout plus 20–30 s of reset ≈ 1 min each, so about 150 rollouts in 2.5 hours of work, leaving breaks and a battery change. 4 policies × 10 starts × 4 repeats = 160: slightly too many; take 4 × 10 × 3 = 120 now and a second session for the fourth repeat, keeping the schedule blocked across sessions. Warm-up rollouts discarded and declared in advance; policy launch and latency checks done before the session starts.
6. Agreed: it is internal validity, not generality. It shows which policy is better on this setup, with a fair protocol. Generality needs varied conditions (positions, lighting, distractors, a second object set), ideally declared as evaluation axes with a few starts each, and reported separately. I would say exactly that in the report, and show the per-condition breakdown.
