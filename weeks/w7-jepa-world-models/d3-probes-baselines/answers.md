# Model answers

1. The information is present but encoded nonlinearly. (a) Planning with latent L2 suffers: distances in latent don't reflect distances in the world (you'll measure this on Friday). (b) A linear reward head on the latent can't read it: needs an MLP head. (c) A VLA's attention/MLPs can decode nonlinear features, but learning to do so costs data.
2. Frames within an episode are near-duplicates; a frame split lets the probe memorise episode-specific appearance, inflating R². Held-out episodes (ideally held-out layouts/sessions) measure generalisation.
3. The same architecture with random weights, probed the same way. For images, random conv features already encode position and colour linearly to a surprising degree (random features are rich), so "R² 0.8" can be mostly architecture, not learning. Report learned vs random-init.
4. With the denominator computed from predictions, any probe whose predictions have the right spread scores high regardless of accuracy, and near-constant predictions explode the ratio: the metric no longer compares against "predict the mean of Y". Sanity check: predicting the mean must give R² ≈ 0.
5. Horizon 10 (autoregressive rollout, compared with copy-last at the same horizon): at horizon 1 slow scenes make copy-last nearly perfect and differences tiny; long horizons reveal whether the dynamics are learned and whether errors compound. Report both, normalised by the copy-last error.
6. It shows the latent ENCODES gripper position, not that the model PREDICTS it under actions, nor that it plans or evaluates well. Missing: prediction error vs copy-last at several horizons, action-conditional counterfactual tests (does changing the action change the predicted gripper position correctly?), and the target role (planning success or failure-detection AUROC vs baselines).
