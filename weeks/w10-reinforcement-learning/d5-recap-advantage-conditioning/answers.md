# Model answers

1. (1) Collect demos, autonomous rollouts and human interventions. (2) Train a value function on all of it (in π*0.6: a distributional value predicting steps-to-success). (3) Compute per-step advantages and binarise them with a per-task threshold. (4) Train the VLA on all data with the indicator as an extra input, with dropout of the condition. (5) Deploy conditioned on "positive", optionally with classifier-free-guidance-style sharpening; repeat. The RL part is policy evaluation (the value function) plus policy improvement through conditioning: the policy extracted is better than the average of the data.
2. Exponential weights have high variance, are dominated by a few samples, need a temperature, and effectively shrink the dataset: hard on a huge flow-matching model. Conditioning keeps a plain supervised loss with every sample at weight 1, works with any generative head, and lets the model learn from bad data what bad looks like.
3. Advantage scale depends on task length and difficulty, so a global threshold would label easy tasks good and hard tasks bad. The value function must also know the task (language instruction) and enough state to normalise by horizon; π*0.6 normalises values per task by the maximum episode length.
4. Failed and sub-optimal autonomous rollouts (labelled I = 0 mostly, with their good parts labelled 1), and human interventions (the corrective segments are forced to "positive"). Demos give the prior, rollouts give coverage of the states the policy actually visits, interventions give recoveries.
5. (a) A poor value function gives noise labels: check its correlation with held-out returns and success. (b) The indicator leaks something else (task, operator, time): check that positives are balanced per task and that I = 1 vs I = 0 changes behaviour within one task. (c) Data collected by a much worse policy has few good actions to extract: improvement saturates; track the paired real success rate per iteration, with confidence intervals.
6. Label success per rollout, fit V on demos + rollouts (steps-to-success target), compute n-step advantages, top 30% per task → I = 1, all demo steps → 1. Fine-tune the policy with the indicator (dropout 0.3). Baselines: the same policy fine-tuned on the same data without the indicator, and filtered BC (successful rollouts only). Evaluate all three on the same 30 fixed starts, paired, with Wilson intervals and McNemar; pre-register the bar (for example +15 points over plain fine-tuning).
